Cards You Can Play

Autolifetech (Clement) ↔ SmartSpace (Yvonne) — recovering payment for completed 2025 renovation works while protecting your position.

Snapshot as of 18 July 2026 · strategy note, not legal advice

A "card" is a discrete move — a message, a step, a decision — that changes the board. They are grouped by escalation tier. The golden rule up front: your strongest position is creditor with clean hands — an undisputed debt, a missed written deadline, and documented losses. Almost every card below is designed to press that advantage without ever surrendering it. The cards in the red section forfeit it instantly — do not play them.

The single most important framing: Your leverage is that you add enormous value and can stop adding it. It is not that you can take away or damage what SmartSpace already owns. "I will stop doing new and free work until I'm paid" is powerful and lawful. "I will lock/disable/withhold your systems until I'm paid" is extortion, likely a Computer Misuse offence, and it converts you from the wronged party into the wrongdoer. Stay on the right side of that line and you keep both the moral high ground and the courtroom.

Tier 1 · Soft / Opening

Low escalation, fully reversible, relationship-preserving. Their real job is to build the written record and force clarity. Play these first — always.

S1Consolidated statement of account

One document: total owed for 2025 reno works, one final agreed figure, no more re-formatting.

How: Anchor on the itemised bill (the Zuh/Nabil-style format Yvonne herself asked for). Send as PDF + email, not just WhatsApp.

Escalation: lowReversible

S2"Confirm the amount so I can close my books"

Ask Yvonne to confirm the figure in writing — framed as an accounting housekeeping request, not a confrontation.

Why: A one-line "yes we owe $X" turns an implied debt into an admitted one. She has already never disputed it.

Escalation: lowSets up every hard card

S3Dated demand with a real deadline

Replace "could you help make the alt payment?" with: amount, reason, the missed "All before CNY" promise, and a firm date 14 days out.

How: Neutral, factual, no anger. A demand invites a date; a chase invites "ok will check".

Escalation: lowStarts the clock

S4Same-day written recap after every contact

After each meeting/call: "Confirming what we agreed today: …". Silence in reply becomes evidence.

Why: Kills the rushed-meeting reset loop where verbal agreements evaporate by the next meeting.

Escalation: lowDo this every time

S5Lock in "no deductions, as agreed"

Confirm in writing that the 6 Mar concession stands: no Yeemax 20% commission offset, no "$500 equipment" claim.

Why: She already replied "OK". Pin it before it quietly returns as a bargaining chip.

Escalation: lowDefensive

S6Separate the three threads

Keep (a) 2025 reno debt, (b) ongoing 7-days/month service, and (c) the TechNext↔her-firm invoices in separate messages.

Why: She wins by blending them ("pay your invoice first"). You win by isolating the clean, undisputed debt.

Escalation: lowStructural
Tier 2 · Leverage — withdraw future value

Legitimate pressure built entirely on ceasing to add value. You never touch what they own — you simply stop giving away your time, expertise, and future work for free. Announce, don't sabotage.

L1Stop all free / unbilled work

No more vendor-invoice audits, Tricomp coordination, e-sign chasing, or invoice re-formatting until the debt is settled.

Script: "Happy to pick this up again once the outstanding reno payment is settled." Per the 6 Mar agreement, extra days already bill by invoice — enforce it.

Escalation: mediumReversible

L2Hand vendor coordination back

Stop being the free buffer between SmartSpace and Tricomp / Yeemax / Skymac. Route vendors to Aiveen / Yvonne directly.

Effect: They feel the full weight of unpaid Tricomp, an angry Yeemax, and a stretched Skymac without you absorbing it. This is real, and it's not your doing.

Escalation: mediumWild-card synergy

L3Pause future deliverables

Next reno plans, 5-year-plan updates, new floor plans — future work you haven't been paid for. Decline to produce more until the past is cleared.

Line: "I can't take on the next phase while the last one is unpaid." Note: keep existing deliverables intact (see red lines).

Escalation: mediumReversible

L4Condition the next project on clearing the old one

Yvonne asked you to "send the plans for next Reno works". That request is your leverage: no new project starts until 2025 is paid.

Why it works: She needs your forward involvement more than she needs to win the past. Tie the two together — on your terms, once.

Escalation: mediumStrong

L5Remove the friend discount from future work

All the discounting was given in exchange for prompt payment that never came. Future work reverts to standard commercial rates.

Frame: "The mates' rates were tied to being paid on time — that didn't happen, so going forward it's standard pricing."

Escalation: mediumRepricing

L6Notice to scale back the 7-days/month

Give formal notice that your ongoing monthly service reduces / ends unless arrears and terms are regularised.

How: In writing, with a date. Offer Hanif / a qualified stand-in for handover continuity (you already agreed this is allowed) — professional, not abrupt.

Escalation: med-highNotice period matters

L7Reject the "partial payout" reframing

State plainly that the Jan–Jun monies are payment for completed 2025 works, not a future-service fee that can be reduced or made conditional on who fulfils service.

Why: This is the exact spot (6 Mar) where a fixed debt is being turned into a soft, cancellable one. Refuse it in writing now.

Escalation: mediumDefensive + offensive
Tier 3 · Structural / Documentation

Cards that reshape the board rather than push on it — turning your indispensability and the entanglements into clean, documented positions that a court or mediator can see at a glance.

D1The honest dependency / risk register

A calm, factual document: what systems exist, who depends on you, and what needs attention if you're not around (WiFi, cybersecurity, Home Assistant, RCU, electrical).

Why it's a card, not a threat: It's information, professionally presented. It makes the continuity risk undeniable without you ever saying "or else". Facts do the arguing.

Escalation: mediumInformation ≠ threat

D2Paid, documented handover — offered in exchange for settlement

Offer a proper transition dossier (configs, credentials that are theirs, plans) as a deliverable that completes on settlement.

Key distinction: You are selling an orderly handover, not holding property hostage. Settle → you hand over cleanly and charge for the handover work. This is lawful; withholding what's theirs is not (see red lines).

Escalation: med-highGet IP ownership advice

D3Regularise your own status

Clarify in writing whether you are contractor or employee, what the payroll drip (every 24th) covers, and whether the reno debt is inside or outside it.

Why: The 24 Mar "it's inside your monthly salary" line is being used to blur the lump-sum debt into salary. Un-blur it on paper.

Escalation: mediumClarity

D4De-tangle the cross-invoices

If TechNext genuinely owes Yvonne's firm for corp-sec/accounting, pay it or dispute it on its own merits — then that lever is gone.

Why: Removes the "pay your invoice first" (11 Mar) counter-move. Never accept netting-off unless documented and the reno figure is confirmed first.

Escalation: low-medNeutralises a lever

D5Preserve the full evidence bundle now

Export the complete, unfiltered WhatsApp chat (with media), archive every bill version, the opex submission, the Google Sheet with revision history.

Why: The screenshots are filtered ("payment" search). Do a full export before anything escalates or anyone edits/deletes messages.

Escalation: noneDo this today
Tier 4 · Formal / Hard

The escalation ladder. Each rung is reversible before you climb the next, and each is fully compatible with "I still value the friendship, I just need this closed." Use only after Tier 1 has created the record and a deadline has lapsed.

H1Mediation

Neutral third party (e.g. a community/commercial mediation centre). Often resolves disputes between parties who want to keep the relationship.

When: If she engages but stalls on a date. Low-cost, face-saving, non-public.

Escalation: med-highRelationship-friendly

H2Lawyer's letter of demand

A solicitor's letter stating the amount, the basis, and a payment deadline, with escalation flagged.

Why: Clients who pay other vendors normally often pay immediately on a lawyer's letter. Frequently the whole endgame.

Escalation: highHigh success rate

H3Small Claims Tribunal / civil claim

File a claim sized to the amount. Your record (admitted debt, missed written deadline, no dispute in 6 months) makes it unusually clean.

Note: Check the tribunal's monetary limit and time-bar for your jurisdiction; above the limit it's a civil suit.

Escalation: highGet legal advice on forum

H4Statutory demand (heavy — advice first)

If the debtor is a company and the debt is undisputed and above the threshold, a formal statutory demand is a strong instrument.

Caution: Powerful and aggressive; wrong use (if the debt is genuinely disputed) can backfire. Only on a lawyer's advice.

Escalation: very highLawyer-gated
Wild cards · Situational

Context-specific plays that don't sit on the escalation ladder. Powerful, but each carries a catch — read the fine print before playing.

W1Let the vendors apply their own pressure

Tricomp (unpaid), Yeemax (angry at re-checking a 6-month-old invoice), Skymac (stretched) are already unhappy — with SmartSpace, not you.

Play: Stop shielding Yvonne from it (see L2). You don't stir it up — you simply stop absorbing it. Their pressure is independent and credible.

Escalation: mediumDon't weaponise — just stop buffering

W2The genuine walk-away

Real, credible willingness to end the ongoing arrangement (with proper notice + orderly handover) is your ultimate leverage.

Why it only works if real: Bluffing a walk-away to someone who runs out the clock in meetings fails. Be ready to actually do it.

Escalation: highMust be genuine

W3Deadline + stated consequence, then follow through

One clear offer, one date, one consequence ("after [date], discounts are withdrawn and I instruct a lawyer"). Then actually do it.

Why: The whole pattern relies on you never enforcing. Enforcing once resets every future interaction in your favour.

Escalation: mediumCredibility card

W4The reputational card — handle with tongs

You share a professional/social network. How SmartSpace treats vendors is, factually, known in that network.

Caution: Never threaten to damage reputation and never spread anything untrue — that invites a defamation claim and burns the friendship for good. At most, be honest if directly asked by peers. Lowest-priority card; easy to misplay.

Escalation: highLegal + relationship risk

02Cards you must not play

These feel tempting because your operational control is total. Every one of them destroys your greatest asset — clean hands — and can turn a debt you're owed into a crime you committed. Off the table, permanently.

The red lines

  • ✕Do not withhold, change, or "lose" passwords/access to systems SmartSpace owns. That's the difference between a creditor and someone committing unauthorised-access / extortion. It hands Yvonne a police report and a counterclaim.
  • ✕Do not disable, degrade, throttle, or "let fail" anything. WiFi, cybersecurity, Home Assistant, the Room Control Unit, electrical — no sabotage, no engineered outages, no "accidentally" breaking things. Even letting something fail on purpose is sabotage.
  • ✕Do not delete or corrupt configs, plans, floor plans, or data. Retaining your own working files/IP until paid may be defensible — destroying anything is not. Get advice on who owns what before touching it.
  • ✕Do not misuse the accounting-system access you were given. You have access to their finance system for legitimate work. Using it for anything else, or removing/altering data, is a serious offence.
  • ✕Do not plant traps, backdoors, kill-switches, or time-bombs. Anything designed to break later is criminal intent, and it will be found in discovery.
  • ✕Do not make the "equipment might have been taken" thing reciprocal. Don't counter with your own accusations of theft or bad faith you can't prove. Keep it clean: you're owed money for work done, full stop.
  • ✕Do not threaten to leak data or reputation-bomb. Threats-for-payment is blackmail/extortion regardless of how you're owed. State facts if asked; never trade silence for money.

The clean-hands principle: withholding your future labour is lawful leverage. Withholding or harming their property is not. When a card is ambiguous, ask: "Am I refusing to give more, or am I taking away what's theirs?" Only the first kind is safe to play.
