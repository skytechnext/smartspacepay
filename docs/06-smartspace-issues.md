# SmartSpace — Root-Cause Issue Profile

The payment problem is a symptom. The underlying condition is a pattern of
**owner indecision, shifting agreements, and avoidance** that makes the project
un-runnable and payment perpetually deferrable. Documenting the pattern matters
for three reasons:

1. It explains *why* every payment model and deadline collapses — it is not
   bad luck, it is a repeating behaviour.
2. Several items are **operational and safety risks** (e.g. works left
   incomplete on a hypothetical "big reno" assumption), which create liability
   that should be put in writing to protect Autolifetech.
3. Each issue maps to specific remedies / cards in
   [05-cards-to-play.html](05-cards-to-play.html).

---

## The eight core issues

### 1. Flaky and indecisive owner decisions
- No one — vendors included — knows the real timeline of the renovation works.
- Decisions are made and then reversed countless times.
- **Example:** door-access location has changed at essentially every meeting.
- **Example:** no decision on doors / door frames at all.
- **Effect:** vendors are confused; work can't be sequenced; nothing reaches
  "done", so nothing reaches "payable".

### 2. Flaky agreements — the terms keep changing
- Agreements don't hold. This is the same drift documented in the billing
  saga (hourly → itemised → categories → cost-plus → monthly fee), but it
  applies to scope and design decisions too.
- **Effect:** no stable baseline exists to deliver against or invoice against.

### 3. Recurring personal excuses
- "Kids are sick", "I'm very busy with xyz", grandma can't pick up the kids,
  scheduling can't be met.
- **Effect:** meetings and decisions slip indefinitely; the excuse is always
  individually reasonable, but the *pattern* is the problem.

### 4. "Let's pay" → then admin / accountant becomes the blocker
- The owner verbally agrees to pay, then the next message re-routes it through
  Aiveen (files to dig, medical leave, "pay your invoice first", payroll
  timing).
- **Effect:** a moving third-party gate that the owner can invoke at will and
  is never resolved. (See the gatekeeper pattern in `02-analysis.md` §4.)

### 5. Reminder fatigue — too many payment reminders already
- Payment has been chased repeatedly across Jan–Jul 2026 (see
  `04-evidence-log.md`).
- **Effect:** the relationship is being spent on chasing; each additional
  soft reminder now yields less. This is the signal to switch from *reminders*
  to a *dated demand with a stated consequence* (Cards S3 / W3).

### 6. Works held back on an unconfirmed "Big Reno" assumption
- Work is deliberately left incomplete — e.g. **hanging wires** — on the
  assumption that a larger renovation will later happen.
- **Risk:** this is not just inefficiency. Incomplete/exposed electrical work
  left live or unterminated is a **safety and liability exposure**. If the big
  reno keeps not being decided (see Issue 1), these interim states persist
  indefinitely.
- **Action:** put the safety-critical items in writing to the owner now, with
  a recommendation to either complete or make-safe, so liability for the
  interim state is documented as flagged and owner-directed. (Card D1 — the
  risk register.)

### 7. Owner unavailable for meetings / chats
- The owner is hard to reach; Autolifetech is "usually delayed" waiting.
- Meetings, when they happen, are rushed and agendas never complete.
- **Effect:** decisions and payment both stall behind availability. Remedy:
  move decisions to **writing/email** where they can't be blocked by
  unavailability or reset by running out the clock (Card S4).

### 8. Decision-making has been removed from Autolifetech
- During the renovation Autolifetech was **proactive** — driving decisions,
  sequencing, sourcing.
- It is now **reactive** — waiting on the owner for calls it used to be
  trusted to make.
- **Effect:** the one party able to keep the project moving has been
  sidelined, which feeds Issues 1, 2 and 6. Restoring a defined decision
  mandate (in writing, with clear boundaries) is itself a negotiating ask.

## Pattern summary

| Theme | Issues | What it produces |
|---|---|---|
| Indecision | 1, 2, 8 | No stable scope, no timeline, nothing reaches "done" |
| Avoidance | 3, 7 | Meetings and decisions slip indefinitely |
| Payment deferral | 4, 5 | Verbal yes, structural no; reminder fatigue |
| Deferred completion | 6 | Interim states persist → **safety / liability risk** |

## Why this strengthens Autolifetech's position

- The flakiness is **documentable** and largely already in writing. It shifts
  the narrative from "vendor wants money" to "owner cannot run a stable
  project or honour agreements", which is exactly the context a mediator or
  court needs to understand the non-payment.
- Issue 6 gives a **legitimate, non-adversarial reason to force decisions and
  completion**: safety. "I need a decision on X because the interim state is a
  liability" is hard to keep deferring and is squarely in the owner's interest.
- Issue 8 reframes a concession Autolifetech can *ask for* (a defined decision
  mandate) as the fix to the owner's own problem — useful leverage that also
  genuinely helps the project.
